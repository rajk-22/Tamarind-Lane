Why we opened Tamarind Lane

We wanted a restaurant that cooks South Indian food the way we eat it at home, but with the care of a kitchen that has something to prove.

Tamarind Lane started as a Sunday supper club in a rented flat. Twelve people, one long table, a menu that changed with whatever Meera found at the market. The waiting list got longer than the table, so we found a space on 12th Main and opened the doors.

The menu is still short on purpose. We'd rather cook fourteen things well than forty things quickly.

How we cook

Ferment slowly

Idli and dosa batter rests overnight. Pickles and chutneys are made in small batches, never bought in.

Grind daily

Spices are roasted and ground each morning, so the pepper in your roast still smells like pepper.

Buy close

Vegetables from farms around Bengaluru, fish from the Karnataka coast, rice from Tamil Nadu.
